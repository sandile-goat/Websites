The short version. We keep your file for as long as we are working with you. If no work follows, we delete it within seven days and empty the trash. We do not sell anything to anyone, and we do not use your data to write anything public unless you tick a box asking us to.

What we collect

When you enquire

Your name, your email address, and whatever you write in the message. Nothing else, and there is no tracking on this site that follows you anywhere.

When you send an export

Whatever your system puts in the file. We do not ask you to prepare or strip it first, because asking people to clean a file they have not read is how mistakes happen.

That means the file will often contain personal information about other people. A claims export usually carries patient numbers, dates of service, diagnosis and procedure codes. A point of sale export usually carries operator identifiers, which identify your staff. A cargo export usually carries less, but may carry driver or handler references.

We treat all of it as personal information, including identifiers that are only numbers. A claims file with patient numbers in it is not anonymous. It is coded, and codes can be matched back by whoever holds the key. We will not describe such a file as anonymous or de-identified, and you should be wary of anyone who does.

Why we have it, and what we do with it

One purpose: to answer the questions you asked us about your own business. We read the file, count and compare what is in it, and give you back what it says.

We do not need to know who your patients or customers are in order to do that, and we do not attempt to find out. Nothing we do involves matching your file against any other source.

Where you are a health care provider sending claims data, you remain the responsible party for that information and we act as your operator. That relationship should be recorded in writing between us before you send anything, and we will raise it rather than wait for you to.

Who else sees it

Two categories of people, and no others.

  • The people working on your engagement. That is a small number and you will know their names.
  • Google, because the upload form and the storage behind it are Google services. Your file sits in Google's infrastructure from the moment you send it until we delete it. Google acts as our operator and does not get to use your file for its own purposes, but it does hold a copy, and you should know that before you upload rather than after.

We do not sell, rent, share or trade your data. There is no advertising on this site and no third party receives your information for marketing. If we are ever legally compelled to hand something over, we will tell you unless we are prohibited from telling you.

How long we keep it

  • No engagement follows. The file is deleted within seven days of us giving you the free read, and the trash is emptied so no recoverable copy remains.
  • An engagement follows. The file is kept for as long as the work runs, then deleted on the same terms when it ends.
  • Enquiry messages. Kept while we are in conversation and for a reasonable period after, so we can pick up where we left off.
  • Our own findings. The summary we wrote for you is ours to keep, and it contains no personal information. Where it contains anything traceable to an individual, it is deleted with the file.

If you come back after your file has been deleted, send it again. It took two minutes the first time.

Research and published writing

We write about what we learn in these sectors. At present that writing draws on public sources and our own reading, not on client files.

If we want to use patterns from your data in something published, we ask first. The consent box on our form is unticked by default and you are free to leave it that way. Saying no changes nothing about the work we do for you, and there is no version of our service that costs more because you declined.

Where you do agree, we would use aggregate patterns only, and nothing that identifies your practice, your shop, your staff, your patients or your customers. We would never publish a named client without asking you separately, in writing, for that specific piece.

Where it is stored

On Google's infrastructure, which may mean servers outside South Africa. POPIA permits transfers across borders where the receiving party is bound by comparable protection, which Google's data processing terms provide. If cross-border storage is a problem for your own compliance position, tell us before you send anything and we will find another way.

How it is protected

Access is limited to the people doing the work. Transfers happen over the upload form rather than email, because email copies a file into several mailboxes and leaves it there.

We will not claim to be unbreachable. If a breach affects your data, we will tell you and the Information Regulator as POPIA requires, and we will tell you what we actually know rather than a reassuring version of it.

Your rights

Under POPIA you may ask us what personal information we hold about you, ask us to correct it, ask us to delete it, object to how we use it, and withdraw a consent you previously gave. Withdrawing the research consent is a one-line email and takes effect immediately.

Write to the Information Officer at [REPLACE_ME]. If we handle your request badly you may complain to the Information Regulator of South Africa, which you can do without going through us first.

Where you are a practice or a business asking on behalf of a patient or customer whose information was in your file: come to us and we will help you answer them, since it is your responsibility and our file.

Changes

If we change how any of this works, we change this page, and we tell anyone whose file we are currently holding. We will not quietly broaden what we do with data already sent under an older version of this notice.

Anything here you want explained

Ask before you send a file rather than after. We would rather answer a hard question about data handling than receive an export from somebody who was uneasy about sending it.
