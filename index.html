Questions

Before you send anything

What if the export does not have much in it?

Then you get less, and we tell you what was missing and which column would have produced it.

Some things come back from any export: how many records there are, what period they cover, how complete the records are, and whether anything is recorded twice. Amounts add where your revenue is concentrated and what is going out in discounts. Payment dates add what is unpaid, how old it is, and how long payment usually takes. Item lines add what has stopped moving.

Do we have to send you patient or customer details?

No names, no ID numbers, no contact details. Take those columns out before you send it.

What the numbers do need is a consistent code, so the same account reads as the same account across twelve months. Whether code 4471 belongs to a name stays on your system. Scheme codes, item codes, dates and amounts carry the rest.

If your system will not export without the name column, open the file, delete that column and save it before sending. Nothing we do needs it.

Some of what comes back is about how the system is used rather than what was sold. We report that as a finding about the system, not about a person.

What happens to the data after we send it?

It goes through an upload form hosted by Google, into storage behind it that may sit outside South Africa. Google holds a copy alongside us until we delete it. No analytics service and no advertiser ever sees it.

A few people have access to it. That is the whole company, so the list is short and it does not change.

You send it through the upload form, not by email. We keep it for as long as we are working with you, then delete it. If we read it and nothing comes of it, it is deleted within seven days and the trash is emptied. The full notice.

What does this cost?

We do not publish a figure, because the work is not the same size twice.

Two things move it: how many systems we are reading, and whether you want them read once or kept running. Both are things you decide, not things we discover afterwards.
